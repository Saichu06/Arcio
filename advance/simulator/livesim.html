    How To Run This Mission

    Manual

    A quick walkthrough of the simulator — from wiring the automation to deploying the finished smart city. Everything below maps directly to the panels on this page.

    01

    Read the objective

    Check the Mission Control panel on the left. Your goal is to keep City Comfort Index above 70% while using the least grid power possible — everything you do is judged against that.

    02

    Wire the hardware

    In the Arduino Control Center on the right, flip the switches to wire the Relay and the Cooling Fan Bank — these are required. Street Light Driver, Display Board, LED and Siren are optional but add efficiency and safety.

    03

    Set the response rule

    Drag the temperature threshold slider to decide when cooling should activate, and leave Flood-safe lockdown checked so non-essential power cuts automatically if flood risk spikes.

    04

    Press Run Simulation

    Click Run Simulation in the console bar. The mission clock starts, live weather drives the city, and the Arduino begins making its own cooling decisions each tick.

    05

    Watch the digital twin

    The center city scene reacts in real time — traffic lights cycle, street lamps glow at night, fans spin, clouds and rain track live weather. Click any sensor dot to inspect its reading, pin and status.

    06

    Inspect the hardware

    Hover the buildings, traffic light, weather station, turbine, solar panel, lake, drainage or park for live engineering data. Double-click the Arduino board to open the fullscreen GPIO visualizer.

    07

    Stress-test with events

    Use the event buttons (Heatwave, Storm, Poor AQI, Power Failure, etc.) in the console to throw curveballs and see whether your wiring and thresholds hold up.

    08

    Review & deploy

    When the mission ends, a summary shows your score, power saved and AI rating. Click Explain in the console for reasoning, then Deploy Smart City to keep it running live.

    Good To Know
    Live Telemetry panelSix sparkline graphs below the console track temperature, humidity, AQI, rain probability, wind and power reserve tick-by-tick.
    Mission TimelineA narrative, human-readable log of major events — separate from the raw system console below it.
    IoT Cloud Status widgetThe floating card bottom-right shows Arduino connectivity, packet count, MQTT and latency — click its header to collapse it.
    Ambient soundThe speaker icon in the top bar turns on wind, rain, fan hum and relay clicks — off by default, tap to enable.
    Mission progress gaugeThe ring next to the title tracks overall mission completion — same number as the progress bar in Mission Control.
    Data-flow particlesBlue dots are routine sensor readings, orange is a command from the Arduino, red means a sensor or network fault is active.
