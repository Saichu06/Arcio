Aim
To combine a push button, an LDR light sensor, an LED, and a buzzer on a single ESP32 to build a small integrated smart-home scene that reacts to both manual input and ambient light.
Objective
Read a digital push button and an analog LDR sensor at the same time, and drive an LED and a buzzer with priority logic: a manual button press always turns on the LED and sounds the buzzer, while — if the button is not pressed — darkness alone turns on the LED (silently). This demonstrates how a single microcontroller combines multiple inputs into one coordinated automation decision.
Components Used
  • ESP32 (Dual-core Tensilica, WiFi-enabled, digital + ADC I/O)
  • Push Button (digital input, GPIO 4)
  • LDR — Light Dependent Resistor (analog input, GPIO 34)
  • LED (output, GPIO 5)
  • Passive Buzzer (output, GPIO 12)
  • 220Ω resistor (LED) · 10kΩ resistor (LDR voltage divider)
  • Breadboard & Jumper Wires
Procedure
  • Place an ESP32 on the canvas from the Component Library
  • Place a Push Button and connect it to ESP32 GPIO 4
  • Place an LDR sensor and connect it to ESP32 GPIO 34 (analog)
  • Place an LED and connect ESP32 GPIO 5 to it, and a Buzzer connected to GPIO 12
  • Click Analyze Circuit to validate the wiring, then click Run Simulation
  • Press-and-hold the button to force the LED and buzzer ON; release it and drag the LDR's light-level slider below the threshold to see the LED alone respond to darkness
Expected Output
While the button is pressed, GPIO 5 and GPIO 12 both go HIGH — the LED turns ON and the buzzer sounds, regardless of ambient light. When the button is released, the system falls back to checking the LDR: if the light level is below the threshold (dark), GPIO 5 goes HIGH and the LED turns ON while the buzzer stays silent; in bright conditions, both outputs stay LOW/OFF.
Learning Outcome
Students understand how to combine multiple sensor inputs (digital and analog) into a single decision tree with priority ordering, how state management works across several simultaneous inputs and outputs, and how this forms the foundation of real-world smart-home automation scenes.
